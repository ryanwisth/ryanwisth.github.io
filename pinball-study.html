The problem

The main risk in a study like this is trying many measures and reporting the ones that happen to fit. Here the tests and verdicts were fixed in advance.

Decisions I made

  • Predictions before results. Each designer’s rule of thumb was written down with its direction, test and verdict rule, then locked before any measure met a rating.
  • One shared board format. A new design is measured exactly like a converted commercial table.
  • Borrow the physics, don’t invent it. A hand-tuned flipper was replaced with one from an established pinball simulator.
  • Year and maker always counted. Era and brand are never mistaken for “fun”.
  • Trust only what holds up. The expert simulated player didn’t reliably beat the medium one, so every prediction uses the medium player.

How it was tested

  • Every table predicted unseen, then compared with simply guessing the average.
  • Two measures of success, public rating and how many tables were made, each with error ranges.
  • The locked predictions are stored with the results.

Limits

  • Ratings mostly reward theme, license, how deep the game’s rules go, sound and software, which a 2D simulator can’t see.
