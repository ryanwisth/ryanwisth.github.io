The problem

A “source” on a big family-history website is often just another user’s tree. Here every new person, parent link or date must quote an original record, match the person on at least two details, and survive a check built to disprove it.

Decisions I made

  • AI suggests; code checks what code can check. Case files, the filter and short review sheets moved from AI helpers into simple code.
  • A second AI tries to disprove. Its rule: when you can’t confirm, reject.
  • A free filter between the two rounds of AI. It drops statements that name no record, match on fewer than two details, repeat a known fact or add a living person.
  • Quotes are matched loosely, not word for word. Capitals, accents and punctuation are ignored, and a miss goes to the skeptic instead of being dropped.
  • Nothing is deleted. The original file is never edited, so every change traces to its round and entry.
  • Offline by default. Going online must be switched on, with a pause between requests and a cap on how many.

How it was tested

  • Runs offline, start to finish, with one command, on a made-up family of 420 people.
  • Merges lose nothing. The scan found all 3 planted duplicates, and the checker proved no merged person lost a fact.
  • The filter drops what it should: a duplicate child, its parent link, a living person and a fact citing only an online tree.
  • Every original line survives the rebuild, and every link inside the file still works.

Limits

  • Every person, place and record in the public demo is made up; the round results come from the private tree.
  • The public code works from saved record pages that the user provides.
