A study app built around mistakes

A study app that groups questions by the mistake they test, and practices each mistake until it is fixed, on time. Timed sets are graded on pacing as well as score.

Problem
Practicing by topic treats every wrong answer on “percents” the same. But most come from one specific error: adding two percent changes instead of multiplying them, or averaging times instead of adding rates.
Result
A working app that practices each mistake until it is mastered: 3 correct, on-time answers in a row on 3 different questions. It checks again after 3, 7 and 21 days, so mastery has to last. Timed sets are graded on pacing as well as score. It runs from 2 files with nothing to install, and loads anyone’s own questions from a spreadsheet file.

The problem

Practicing the whole chapter again never targets that one error. Here, every question is labeled with the mistake it tests. A mistake counts as mastered only after 3 correct, on-time answers on 3 different questions. Checks at 3, 7 and 21 days prove the fix held, and one wrong answer reopens it.

Decisions I made

  • Practice the mistake, not the topic. Each question belongs to one mistake, so practice goes straight at the error.
  • Speed is part of mastery. A slow right answer does not add to the streak. Mastered means right and on time.
  • Mastery needs different questions. The streak has to come from 3 different questions. Getting one question right over and over only proves memory.
  • A missed question is retired. Once its explanation is shown, a later right answer could just be memory, so it never comes up again.
  • Pacing gets its own grade. The report separates time spent on right answers from time lost on wrong ones.

How it was tested

  • I built the sample questions, ran the app, and tried every part: practice, timed sets, progress, pacing and the quality check.
  • All 21 sample questions are checked by a program, and only checked questions are shown.
  • A built-in quality check flags wrong answer keys, repeated choices, near-copies of questions, and mistakes with too few questions. If any row of a question file fails, the whole file is refused.
  • I tested the rules by using the app for my own study.

Scope

  • One user, on your own computer, with no sign-in.
  • The public code is a general version of a larger study tool I built for myself.
