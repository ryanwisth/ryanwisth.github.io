The problem

Asking the AI to “double-check” gives a second confident answer, with no record of what was checked or how.

The first fix, rules written into the chat, worked at first and then wore off. A review measured how far, and that shaped the rebuild.

Decisions I made

  • Put the checklist in code, not in the chat. By the late batches, “best case” was filled in on 0 of 28 corrections, and “what would make it wrong” on 57% of entries. Now one incomplete entry rejects the whole batch and nothing is written, so the checklist cannot drift.
  • Riskiest first. Re-checks go first, then points whose wording changed, then open statements prioritized by importance times weakness, so the most damaging errors show up earliest.
  • Flag the AI checking its own notes. A verdict that rests on the project’s own notes is marked that way and listed for a person to verify.
  • Stopping is a cost decision for a person. The trade-off: batches 10 to 29 were 73% of the work and found problems in 7% of first checks. The tool reports the rate and an estimate of what is left, so the call to stop is made on numbers.

How it was tested

  • Rerunning the first report’s 546 entries through the new review gives the same result as the review done by hand, including the drift it found.
  • A self-test runs 27 start-to-finish checks on a throwaway report and passes on the public copy.
  • The stop rule counts only first checks, after the review showed 42 of 66 early problems came from re-checks.

Limits

  • Works on Markdown reports (plain-text files with simple formatting).
  • The tool enforces the form of every check; the judgment in each verdict stays with the person reviewing.
