Case study: AI inside a physical device

AI feedback written in ink on an e-ink tablet

Press a Grade button on a reMarkable Paper Pro, and an AI model reviews the handwritten work on that page. It writes its feedback back onto the page as real pen strokes, in a separate layer the user can delete.

The problem

Working problems by hand on paper-like e-ink is good for learning. But getting feedback usually means retyping the work into a computer or photographing it.

The goal was to close that loop on the device itself: write, tap Grade, and a few seconds later the margin shows a verdict, the mistake struck through, and the worked continuation from where things went wrong.

What I directed

I set the goals, the safety rules, the acceptance checks and the design specs, and tested each stage on the tablet. The agents wrote most of the code.

  • Never touch the student's ink. Feedback goes into its own "AI Feedback" layer. A free-space finder places each note in empty space or on a continuation page; skipping a mark beats writing over someone's work.
  • Button-triggered only. Nothing grades in the background. The Grade button grades the current page, a set number of pages, or the whole notebook.
  • Describe what the ink shows, not why. The tablet stores strokes in the order they were written, so the reviewer sees the sequence, not only the final page. The prompt forbids guessing why a mistake happened, and statements about motives or feelings are filtered out.
  • Show the work where it is needed. A wrong or blank page gets the full worked continuation. A correct, efficient page gets almost nothing.
  • Legible handwriting over clever handwriting. Neural handwriting generators failed on math symbols and digits, so the feedback uses a single-stroke font with natural pen movement.

How the results were verified

  • Every equation the reviewer wants to write is evaluated in code before it is inked. False ones are dropped.
  • A fingerprint of the student's strokes stops an unchanged page from being graded twice.
  • The full loop (button press, capture, review, ink in the feedback layer) was built and used on the device in August 2026, including 24-page batches.
  • A scale bug that drew ink 18% too large on Letter-size worksheets was traced to the coordinate math and fixed with one shared source for page coordinates.

The outcome

A working grader on one tablet: tap a button, get checked, handwritten-looking feedback on the page in seconds, without the student's own writing ever being changed.

The public version leaves out the private question bank. Without it, the reviewer grades from the handwriting alone and is told it cannot see the question.

Honest limits

  • Tested on one device and one firmware version, 3.27.3. The extension stack is tied to that firmware.
  • The Letter-size scale fix was verified numerically, not yet on paper. Zoom handling was verified on synthetic cases only.
  • There is no automated test suite in the repository; the original test pages contained private handwriting.
  • The project is paused, and the grading service is not currently running.
