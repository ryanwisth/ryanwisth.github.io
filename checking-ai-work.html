Case study: trust in AI output

Making AI work checkable

One principle applied in three tools: the AI proposes, code enforces the rules, and a second pass tries to refute the first.

The problem

AI output reads well and is wrong in places, and nobody can tell where by reading it. Asking the model to "double-check" gives a second confident pass with no record of what was checked or how.

Written rules do not fix this. In my own sessions the agent agreed with the rules and broke them an hour later.

What I directed

In each tool I set the goals, the rules and the acceptance checks. The agents wrote most of the code.

  • Research: genealogy-evidence-pipeline. Finder agents only propose additions to a family tree. Every claim must cite and quote a primary record. A free Python gate drops what breaks the rules, then a skeptic agent reviews the rest with the stance "when you cannot confirm, reject". Accepted facts go into an append-only ledger.
  • Reports: claim-ledger. Every point in a long report gets a stable ID, a testable statement, what would make it wrong, quoted evidence and a verdict. The riskiest points go first. The record template is enforced in code: one incomplete record rejects the whole batch.
  • Cost: claude-code-token-guard. Hooks deny the wasteful tool call, such as a subagent spawned with no model named, and the denial message says what to do instead. The agent corrects itself on the next call.

How the results were verified

  • Research. The public copy runs end to end offline on an invented 420-person family. In that run the gate kept 12 of 16 claims and dropped 4, including a living person and a fact citing only an online tree.
  • Reports. Replaying the first ledger's 546 records through the tool reproduces its hand-made audit. A 27-check self-test passes on the published copy.
  • Cost. Each hook followed a leak measured in my session logs, and I checked that it changed the numbers in the following week's scan. 15 sample cases cover the allow and deny paths.

The outcome

  • The research pipeline ran three rounds on a private tree in September 2026. On that tree the skeptics rejected 31 claims from a fast first pass and 3 of 147 from a careful one.
  • The first report check gave a practical answer to "how much checking is enough": batches 1 to 9 found problems in 27% of first checks, and batches 10 to 29 found 7% while taking 73% of the work.
  • The claim ledger and the token guard have been in daily use since October 2026.

Honest limits

  • The same model writes and checks. The claim ledger labels a verdict that rests only on the project's own notes, and lists those points for a person to verify.
  • The research agent scripts were not re-run after the public copy was scrubbed; they pass a syntax check only.
  • The token guard has no automated test suite, and its thresholds were tuned for one person's usage.
