Trust in AI output

Making AI work checkable

One principle applied in three tools: the AI proposes, code enforces the rules, and a second pass tries to refute the first.

Problem
AI output reads well and is wrong in places, and nobody can tell where by reading it.
Result
The research pipeline ran three rounds on a private tree in September 2026; the claim ledger and the token guard have been in daily use since October 2026.

The problem

Asking the model to "double-check" gives a second confident pass with no record of what was checked or how.

Written rules do not fix this. In my own sessions the agent agreed with the rules and broke them an hour later.

Decisions I made

  • Research: genealogy-evidence-pipeline. Every claim must cite and quote a primary record. A free Python gate drops what breaks the rules, then a skeptic agent reviews the rest.
  • Reports: claim-ledger. Every point in a long report gets a stable ID, a testable statement, what would make it wrong, quoted evidence and a verdict. The record template is enforced in code: one incomplete record rejects the whole batch.
  • Cost: claude-code-token-guard. Hooks deny the wasteful tool call, and the denial message says what to do instead.

Each tool also has its own case study: the evidence pipeline, the claim ledger and the token guard.

How it was verified

  • Research. The public copy runs end to end offline on an invented 420-person family. In that run the gate kept 12 of 16 claims and dropped 4.
  • Reports. Replaying the first ledger's 546 records through the tool reproduces its hand-made audit. Batches 1 to 9 found problems in 27% of first checks, and batches 10 to 29 found 7% while taking 73% of the work.
  • Cost. Each hook followed a leak measured in my session logs, and I checked that it changed the numbers in the following week's scan.

Honest limits

  • The same model writes and checks; the claim ledger labels a verdict that rests only on the project's own notes.
  • The research agent scripts were not re-run after the public copy was scrubbed; they pass a syntax check only.
  • The token guard has no automated test suite, and its thresholds were tuned for one person's usage.
